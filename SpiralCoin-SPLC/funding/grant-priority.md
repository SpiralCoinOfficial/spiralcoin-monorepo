## Tips that raise win-rate

1. **Ship before you ask.** Each application should link to a working deployment (testnet OK), real GitHub commits, and a demo URL. SpiralCoin has all three — use them prominently.

2. **Be specific about milestones.** Grant committees fund milestones, not vibes. Each application should propose 2-4 milestones with dates and verifiable deliverables (deployed contract address, audit report URL, user count screenshot).

3. **Show multi-chain commitment to each chain.** Arbitrum wants to see you on Arbitrum; Polygon wants you on Polygon. Tailor each application to make that chain feel like your primary, not "one of six."

4. **Ask for less than the max.** Asking for $25K-$50K wins more often than asking for $250K. You can always re-apply for a follow-on.

5. **Public goods framing.** "SpiralCoin paymaster lets any ERC-4337 wallet pay gas in any token, open-source and free for the ecosystem" wins more than "SpiralCoin paymaster for SPLC users."

6. **Engage publicly.** Comment on the foundation's Twitter, attend their AMAs, contribute to their Discord. Recognition raises P(win) materially.

7. **Disclose risks honestly.** Foundations have lost trust in projects that hid problems. Saying "external audit pending, not deployed to mainnet yet" is fine; lying about it is fatal.

8. **Follow up politely after 2-3 weeks** if no response. Once. Not more.

## What NOT to do

- ❌ Don't apply to all 8 in the same week with copy-paste content. Foundations talk.
- ❌ Don't quote unrealistic TVL/user projections. They'll be checked.
- ❌ Don't claim partnerships you haven't formalized.
- ❌ Don't accept a grant whose terms require selling tokens before your TGE and securities review.
- ❌ Don't accept restricted-use grants that conflict with your roadmap (e.g., "must launch on chain X within 30 days").
